# Author Observation — Host-Derived Motif Causality

Date: 2026-09-07
Owner: Author / Overall SOL
Status: CROSS-LANE AUTHOR CRITERION

## Observation

The current motif placement can read as mechanically random: motifs may cover the Host surface convincingly, but there is not yet a strong reason why a motif exists at one location rather than another.

The author wants the placement to become a consequence of the Host geometry and the generative history rather than an arbitrary surface scatter.

USAGI is a useful test case because its recognizable form suggests an underlying spatial organization that can be read before any motifs are placed.

## Desired story

A preferred causal story is:

> Host geometry -> inferred internal skeleton / medial organization -> sparse permanent branches grow through the volume -> branches approach or reach the surface -> motifs bloom from those consequences.

The final surface should **not** look like a literal tree with one trunk, obvious branches, and leaves/flowers at the tips.

Instead:

- growth may start from multiple internal / structural regions;
- branches may split, reconnect, cross-link, and form loops;
- some branches may run laterally or obliquely through the volume rather than radially outward;
- surface attraction / local competition / spacing may determine where growth reaches the Host boundary;
- motif sites should inherit a reason from the branch / field / growth history;
- the resulting motif distribution may still look irregular or almost random when seen only from the outside;
- that visual irregularity should be an emergent consequence, not the primary placement rule.

Short formulation:

> The motifs may look randomly scattered on the surface, but they should be there because the Host grew them there.

## Relationship to the volumetric structure criterion

This criterion complements `AUTHOR_OBSERVATION_VOLUMETRIC_INTERNAL_STRUCTURE_2026-09-06.md`.

The same internal Permanent Structure may eventually serve several roles at once:

- articulate the Host-derived internal organization;
- create volumetric depth and retained Void;
- contribute to print-causal permanence / load paths;
- generate or condition motif placement;
- preserve the computational specificity seen in A2.

This is preferable to treating Permanent Structure, Support, and motif scatter as unrelated downstream systems.

## What should not happen

Do not reduce this to:

- uniform random points on the surface;
- Poisson / blue-noise spacing with no Host-derived cause;
- curvature-only decoration with no internal history;
- a simple medial-axis tree copied directly to the surface;
- a conventional botanical trunk/branch/leaf hierarchy;
- randomness as the main explanation for placement.

Randomness may remain as bounded variation or a tie-breaker, but it should not be the primary reason a motif exists at a location.

## Candidate causal signals to research

Possible signals include combinations of:

- medial axis / skeletal graph / local thickness;
- interior distance-field ridges;
- geodesic distance and surface reachability;
- curvature / concavity / feature scale;
- diffusion / transport / flow fields through the volume;
- print-causal lower-to-higher growth state;
- Void preservation / sight-line pressure;
- local branch competition and inhibition;
- anastomosis / redundancy history;
- surface contact / emergence events from the permanent graph.

No single signal is prescribed by this observation.

## Author review questions

For future USAGI / AB / C studies ask:

1. Can the motif placement be explained from the Host and growth history without referring to arbitrary random scatter?
2. Does the internal network feel derived from the Host while still having independent generative character?
3. Does the final surface remain irregular enough that the causal system is not visually reducible to a simple GH recipe?
4. Does the network avoid becoming a literal tree?
5. Can motif emergence, volumetric structure, and print-causal permanence share one coherent generative story?
